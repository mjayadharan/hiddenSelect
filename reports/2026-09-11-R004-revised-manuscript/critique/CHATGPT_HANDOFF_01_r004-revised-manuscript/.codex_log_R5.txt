### 7. `Λ_R` is not a new horizon beyond the coarse partitions — **ACCEPT**, and I retract my own R4 amendment's justification

`h_k + w_k = τ_{k+1} − τ_{j−1}`, which is strictly increasing in `k` along the run,

partition. So:

  coarse cost `Ĵ_K` is defined on them;
- consequently the R4 claim that the amendment "makes (A1) strictly harder" and "pushes `ΔT_max` to
  100 even though the fine windows are 75 and 95" is also withdrawn: `ΔT_max ≥ 100` is forced by the
  coarse window `{0,100}` in those transitions regardless, since that window is a window of a
  partition under discussion.

### 8. The claim that enlarging `r_X` cannot rescue (A1-i) is false in general — **ACCEPT**, with a **CLARIFY** on what happens if you do enlarge it

**Accept.** R4's (A1) `\readthis` item (ii) says `μ` and `L̃` are "non-decreasing in `r_X`, and for a
polynomial right-hand side strictly increasing", and concludes that (A1-i) is a competition a larger
tube loses. The first half is right (suprema over a growing set), the second is false, and the
conclusion does not follow from either. Counterexamples are immediate: for `f` affine in `x` the
constants are independent of `X` entirely; and for FHN itself the upper logarithmic norm of
`J_f(x;p)` in the Euclidean norm is bounded above globally — the `1 − x_1²` entry decreases without
bound as `|x_1|` grows — so `μ(X × P)` **saturates** at a finite global value once `X` is large
enough, and grows no further. Nor does non-decreasingness of the constants by itself establish that
`Q(r_X)` outgrows `r_X`; that is an inequality between two growth rates and I asserted it without
proving it for the FHN library.

**Resolution.** The item is weakened to what is true without a system-specific proof: *enlarging
`r_X` may fail to rescue (A1-i), because `μ` and `L̃` must be recomputed as suprema over the larger
`X × P` and can grow rapidly, and `Q(ΔT_max)` depends exponentially on `μ(r_X)ΔT_max` while the
right-hand side of (A1-i) grows only linearly in `r_X`. Whether the competition is won or lost is a
property of the specific library and parameter set and is not settled here.* No theorem is claimed.

**Clarify — and for FHN the honest answer makes the picture worse, not better.** Because FHN's
Euclidean `μ` saturates, (A1-i) *is* satisfiable by inflating `r_X`: fix `X` large enough that
`μ = \nmu` and `L̃ = \nLtilde` are the global values, then any `r_X > Q(ΔT_max)diam(P)` works. At
`ΔT_max = 100` that means `r_X > 5.86·10⁵¹ · diam(P)`. Admissibility is bought — and the same
`Q(ΔT_max)` sits inside every conclusion (`ρ`, `ρ̄`, `Δ_K`, `(eq:thm)`, (B2)), so the bounds one then
owns are vacuous by the identical factor. The manuscript states this explicitly: the obstruction is
not that (A1) is unsatisfiable, it is that the region on which it is satisfiable is one where the
theory's conclusions carry no information. That is a sharper and more honest statement than the
false monotonicity claim it replaces. §2 block (H5).

### 9. Several envelope claims mishandle degenerate cases — **ACCEPT**, with a **CLARIFY** enumerating the equality cases in full

**Accept, both.** (i) Lemma `rhobar`(v) says, for `μ < 0`, `ρ̄_s = 2E` *exactly when*
`L̃δ ≤ |μ|E`. At `s = 0` that is false in the "only if" direction: `ρ_0 = 2E` identically, so
`ρ̄_0 = 2E` for every `δ`, including `δ` arbitrarily large. The characterisation is a statement about
`s > 0` and I stated it for `s ≥ 0`. (ii) Remark `rem:rhoplus` says `ρ̄_s = ρ⁺_s` iff `μ ≥ 0` or
`p = p*`; equality also holds at `E = 0` and at `s = 0`.

**Resolution, with the case analysis done rather than asserted.** For `μ < 0`, `s > 0`, writing
`E = ‖η‖_max`, `δ = ‖p − p*‖`, `Q(s) = (L̃/|μ|)(1 − e^{−|μ|s}) > 0`:

`ρ_s − 2E = (1 − e^{−|μ|s})(L̃δ/|μ| − E)`,

whose sign is that of `L̃δ − |μ|E` and is independent of `s > 0`. Hence **(v) for `s > 0`**:
`ρ̄_s = 2E` iff `L̃δ ≤ |μ|E`, and `ρ̄_s = ρ_s` otherwise; and separately `ρ̄_0 = ρ_0 = 2E` always. For
the `ρ⁺` comparison with `μ < 0`: `ρ⁺_s = 2E + Q(s)δ` and `ρ̄_s = max{2E, (1+e^{μs})E + Q(s)δ}`, so

- if `δ = 0`: both equal `2E`;
- if `E = 0`: both equal `Q(s)δ`;
- if `s = 0`: both equal `2E` (since `Q(0) = 0`);
- otherwise (`s > 0`, `E > 0`, `δ > 0`): if `ρ_s ≥ 2E` then `ρ̄_s = (1+e^{μs})E + Q(s)δ < 2E + Q(s)δ`
  strictly, because `e^{μs} < 1` and `E > 0`; if `ρ_s < 2E` then `ρ̄_s = 2E < 2E + Q(s)δ` strictly.

So `ρ̄_s = ρ⁺_s` **iff** `μ ≥ 0` **or** `p = p*` **or** `E = 0` **or** `s = 0`, and the inequality is
strict otherwise. Both statements are rewritten with these qualifications. §2 block (F5).

**Clarify — one consequence worth flagging.** The `s = 0` case is not a curiosity here: `ρ̄_0` is the
value the bound takes at a datum coincident with its launch node, which the half-open convention
`D_k = {t_i ∈ (τ_k, τ_{k+1}]}` excludes from the removal sums but which appears in `g_k` when
`w_k = 0` is formally allowed. The manuscript notes that `w_k > 0` and `h_k > 0` for every
`k ∈ I_R` by construction (a removed node is interior and distinct from its retained neighbours),
so the degenerate branch is never active in Proposition 3 — but the lemma is stated correctly
anyway, since Lemma 3 is quoted elsewhere at `s ≥ 0`.

### 10. Negative linearised window exponents are conflated with a negative uniform `μ` — **ACCEPT**

**Accept, and this is the same error the manuscript spends a whole remark forbidding.** R4's
`rem:rhoplus` motivates keeping `ρ̄` primary by saying the distinction "lives" where the per-window

constant `μ` over `X × P`** that enters the residual proof, and Table 2 reports integrated, weighted,

unlicensed, committed two pages later in its own motivating text.

**Resolution.** The motivation is rewritten to use a genuinely contractive system, constructed and
labelled as an illustration, not drawn from §6: any field with `λ_max(sym J_f(x;p)) ≤ μ < 0`

### 11. The greedy cost accounting omits the number of candidates — **ACCEPT**

**Accept.** R4 block (H) wrote that one greedy step "costs one integration per candidate plus a
re-integration for every already-removed node whose predecessor changed, i.e. `O(|R|)` in the worst
case" — which states the per-candidate recomputation cost and then reports it as the per-step cost,
dropping the candidate count entirely. With `C` retained interior candidates and `|R|` nodes already
removed, testing *every* candidate costs up to `C` times the per-candidate figure.

**Resolution — three figures, given separately.** With `R` the current removal set and `C = |S ∖ R|
− 2` the number of retained interior candidates:

- **per candidate `c`**: `O(1)` coarse-window integrations — one integration over
  `[τ_c^-(R∪{c}), max D_c]`, which yields `g_c` and every `a_i` for `c` — **plus** the recomputation
  of `w_k`, `g_k` and the `a_i` of every already-removed node `k ∈ R` whose retained left neighbour
  changes when `c` is removed. That set is empty when `c` is isolated from `R`, and is the whole
  consecutive run to the right of `c` in the worst case: `O(|R|)`.
- **per greedy step**: `O(C · |R|)` window integrations in the worst case (every candidate adjacent
  to a long removed run), `O(C)` when the removals stay isolated.
- **whole greedy schedule** of `|I_R|` removals: `O(|I_R| · C · |R|)` worst case, i.e. `O(C|I_R|²)`
  since `|R| ≤ |I_R|`.

**Clarify — caching, stated as a remark and not folded into the headline figure.** Removing a node
`c` changes `τ_k^-` only for nodes lying in the removed run immediately to the right of `c`;
every other candidate's contribution is unchanged and can be cached keyed by the pair
`(τ_k^-(R), τ_k)`. With that cache, a greedy step re-evaluates only the candidates whose left
retained neighbour changed at the previous step, which is `O(1)` candidates for isolated removals and
the affected run otherwise — in practice far below `O(C|R|)`. The worst case above stands as stated,
and the manuscript gives the worst case as the headline figure with caching as the remark, not the
other way round. §2 block (D5).

```latex
\begin{lemma}[local minimiser inequality]\label{lem:vi_local}
Let $U\subseteq\mathbb R^{n_p}$ be convex, $F$ differentiable on a neighbourhood of $U$, and let
$p\in U$ satisfy either $\nabla F(p)=0$, or $\langle\nabla F(p),q-p\rangle\ge0$ for all $q\in U$.
If $F$ is $m$-strongly convex on $U$ then
\begin{equation}
F(q)\ \ge\ F(p)+\tfrac m2\norm{q-p}^2\qquad\text{for all }q\in U .
\label{eq:vi_local}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
```

#### (C5) Proposition 3 — per-datum primary bound, corrected admissibility step, and the corollary chain [replaces R4 block (G) in full]

```latex
% --- notation, placed with \eqref{eq:rho} in \S2.2 ---
For a launch time $\sigma$ and a datum time $t\ge\sigma$ with $t-\sigma\le\Delta T_{\max}$, write
\begin{equation}
r_{\sigma,t}(p)\ =\ e^{\mu(t-\sigma)}\norm{\eta_\sigma}+Q(t-\sigma)\norm{p-\pstar}+\norm{\eta_t},
\label{eq:rdatum}
\end{equation}
the \emph{per-datum residual bound}: it carries the noise realised at the launch node and at the
datum, not the global $\norm\eta_{\max}$. Replacing both by $\norm\eta_{\max}$ gives
$r_{\sigma,t}(p)\le\rho_{t-\sigma}(p)$, and Lemma~\ref{lem:residual} is exactly the statement
$\norm{\flow{t-\sigma}{p}{y_\sigma}-y_t}\le r_{\sigma,t}(p)\le\rho_{t-\sigma}(p)\le\bar\rho_s(p)$
for $t-\sigma\le s$.

\begin{proposition}[cost change under node removal, per datum; \rev{revised}]\label{prop:removal}
\emph{Scope (S3).} Assume (A0)--(A2) and work on $E_X$. Let the coarse partition be a sub-partition
of the fine one, with removed indices $\mathcal I_R\subset\{1,\dots,K-1\}$. For $k\in\mathcal I_R$
put
\[
h_k=\tau_{k+1}-\tau_k,\qquad w_k=\tau_k-\tau_k^{-},\qquad
D_k=\{t_i\in(\tau_k,\tau_{k+1}]\},\qquad
g_k(p)=\big\lVert\flow{w_k}{p}{y_{\tau_k^{-}}}-y_{\tau_k}\big\rVert ,
\]
where $\tau_k^{-}$ is the nearest \emph{retained} node to the left of $\tau_k$. Then for every
$p\in P$
\begin{equation}
\big|\hatJ_K(p)-J_K(p)\big|\ \le\
\sum_{k\in\mathcal I_R}\ \sum_{t_i\in D_k}
e^{\mu(t_i-\tau_k)}\;g_k(p)\;\Big[r_{\tau_k^{-},t_i}(p)+r_{\tau_k,t_i}(p)\Big],
\label{eq:prop_removal_datum}
\end{equation}
and the node factor obeys
\begin{equation}
g_k(p)\ \le\ r_{\tau_k^{-},\tau_k}(p)
\ =\ e^{\mu w_k}\norm{\eta_{\tau_k^{-}}}+Q(w_k)\norm{p-\pstar}+\norm{\eta_{\tau_k}}
\ \le\ \rho_{w_k}(p).
\label{eq:gk}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
\emph{Which data change predictor.} In $J_K$ the datum $t_i\in(\tau_{k-1},\tau_k]$ is launched from
$\tau_{k-1}$; in $\hatJ_K$ from the last \emph{retained} node at or before $\tau_{k-1}$. So $t_i$
changes predictor iff its fine launching node is removed, i.e.\ exactly for
$t_i\in\bigcup_{k\in\mathcal I_R}D_k$. The half-open convention handles the two apparent off-by-one
cases: with $\tau_k$ removed and $\tau_{k-1}$ retained, the datum at time $\tau_k$ lies in
$(\tau_{k-1},\tau_k]$, keeps its predictor, and is correctly \emph{not} in $D_k$; with $\tau_{k-1}$
and $\tau_k$ both removed, the datum at $\tau_k$ lies in $D_{k-1}$, does change predictor, and is
correctly counted there. The $D_k$ are pairwise disjoint.

\emph{Admissibility of every flow evaluation, argued pairwise.} Fix $k\in\mathcal I_R$ and
$t_i\in D_k$, and put
\[
u_i=\flow{t_i-\tau_k^{-}}{p}{y_{\tau_k^{-}}},\qquad
v_i=\flow{t_i-\tau_k}{p}{y_{\tau_k}},\qquad
a_i=\norm{u_i-y_i},\qquad b_i=\norm{v_i-y_i}.
\]
On $E_X$ we have $\norm{y_{\tau_k^{-}}-x^\star(\tau_k^{-})}\le r'$ and
$\norm{y_{\tau_k}-x^\star(\tau_k)}\le r'$, so Lemma~\ref{lem:noescape} applies to each launch
\emph{separately}: the trajectory $s\mapsto\flow{s}{p}{y_{\tau_k^{-}}}$ lies in
$\mathcal T\subseteq X$ for $s\le t_i-\tau_k^{-}\le\Delta T_{\max}$, and
$s\mapsto\flow{s}{p}{y_{\tau_k}}$ lies in $\mathcal T\subseteq X$ for $s\le t_i-\tau_k$. By the flow
property the first of these is the coarse continuation
$\flow{s-\tau_k}{p}{\flow{w_k}{p}{y_{\tau_k^{-}}}}$ for $s\ge\tau_k$ --- a \emph{same-parameter}
continuation, which is why no statement about launches from reachable states is needed. Both
trajectories therefore lie in $X$ on $[\tau_k,t_i]$ and, $X$ being convex, so does the chord joining
them at each time; hence
$T_X\big((\flow{w_k}{p}{y_{\tau_k^{-}}},p),(y_{\tau_k},p)\big)\ge t_i-\tau_k$ and
Lemma~\ref{lem:state} applies to that pair on $[0,t_i-\tau_k]$.

\emph{The per-datum estimate.} $\hatJ_K-J_K=\sum_{k\in\mathcal I_R}\sum_{t_i\in D_k}(a_i^2-b_i^2)$
and $|a_i^2-b_i^2|\le\norm{u_i-v_i}(a_i+b_i)$. Lemma~\ref{lem:state} at the \emph{actual} elapsed
time gives
\[
\norm{u_i-v_i}\ \le\ e^{\mu(t_i-\tau_k)}\,\big\lVert\flow{w_k}{p}{y_{\tau_k^{-}}}-y_{\tau_k}\big\rVert
\ =\ e^{\mu(t_i-\tau_k)}g_k(p),
\]
with the actual $\mu$ and no enlargement of the exponent, hence no monotonicity requirement and no
$\mu^{+}$. Lemma~\ref{lem:residual} applied at node $\tau_k^{-}$ and datum $t_i$ gives
$a_i\le r_{\tau_k^{-},t_i}(p)$, and at node $\tau_k$ and datum $t_i$ gives
$b_i\le r_{\tau_k,t_i}(p)$ --- each at its own elapsed time and with its own realised noise, so
neither an envelope nor the global $\norm\eta_{\max}$ is needed. Summing gives
\eqref{eq:prop_removal_datum}. \eqref{eq:gk} is Lemma~\ref{lem:residual} applied to the single datum
$y_{\tau_k}$ launched from $y_{\tau_k^{-}}$ over elapsed time $w_k$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[uniform-noise form]\label{cor:removal_rho}
Under the hypotheses of Proposition~\ref{prop:removal}, replacing every realised noise norm by
$\norm\eta_{\max}$,
\begin{equation}
\big|\hatJ_K(p)-J_K(p)\big|\ \le\
\sum_{k\in\mathcal I_R}\ \sum_{t_i\in D_k}
e^{\mu(t_i-\tau_k)}\;g_k(p)\;\Big[\rho_{t_i-\tau_k^{-}}(p)+\rho_{t_i-\tau_k}(p)\Big].
\label{eq:prop_removal_node}
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
$r_{\sigma,t}\le\rho_{t-\sigma}$ by \eqref{eq:rdatum} and $\norm{\eta_\bullet}\le\norm\eta_{\max}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[per-node form]\label{cor:removal_node}
Under the same hypotheses,
\begin{equation}
\big|\hatJ_K(p)-J_K(p)\big|\ \le\
\sum_{k\in\mathcal I_R}|D_k|\;e^{\mu^{+}h_k}\;g_k(p)\;
\Big[\bar\rho_{h_k+w_k}(p)+\bar\rho_{h_k}(p)\Big].
\label{eq:prop_removal_pernode}
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
For $t_i\in D_k$: $t_i-\tau_k\le h_k$, so $e^{\mu(t_i-\tau_k)}\le e^{\mu^{+}h_k}$ (with $\mu^{+}$,
because for $\mu<0$ the correct majorant of $e^{\mu(t_i-\tau_k)}$ over $t_i-\tau_k\in[0,h_k]$ is
$e^{0}=1$); $t_i-\tau_k^{-}\le h_k+w_k$ and $t_i-\tau_k\le h_k$, so
$\rho_{t_i-\tau_k^{-}}\le\bar\rho_{h_k+w_k}$ and $\rho_{t_i-\tau_k}\le\bar\rho_{h_k}$ by
Lemma~\ref{lem:rhobar}(i). The inner sum has $|D_k|$ terms.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[collapsed form]\label{cor:removal_global}
Under the same hypotheses, with $n_{\max}=\max_{k\in\mathcal I_R}|D_k|$,
$\Delta T_1=\max_k(\tau_k-\tau_{k-1})$ over the fine partition and
$\Delta T_2=\max_{k\in\mathcal I_R}w_k$,
\begin{equation}
\big|\hatJ_K(p)-J_K(p)\big|\ \le\
n_{\max}\,|\mathcal I_R|\;e^{\mu^{+}\Delta T_1}\;\bar\rho_{\Delta T_2}(p)\;
\Big[\bar\rho_{\Delta T_1+\Delta T_2}(p)+\bar\rho_{\Delta T_1}(p)\Big]\ =:\ \Delta_K(p).
\label{eq:prop_removal}
\end{equation}
At $p=\pstar$ this is
$n_{\max}|\mathcal I_R|e^{\mu^{+}\Delta T_1}\big(1+e^{\mu^{+}\Delta T_2}\big)
\big[\big(1+e^{\mu^{+}(\Delta T_1+\Delta T_2)}\big)+\big(1+e^{\mu^{+}\Delta T_1}\big)\big]
\norm\eta_{\max}^{2}$, quadratic in the noise. Moreover $\Delta_K$ is non-decreasing in
$\delta=\norm{p-\pstar}$, so $\sup_{q\in U}\Delta_K(q)=\Delta_K$ evaluated at
$\delta=R_U:=\sup_{q\in U}\norm{q-\pstar}$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
$h_k\le\Delta T_1$, $w_k\le\Delta T_2$, $|D_k|\le n_{\max}$,
$g_k\le\rho_{w_k}\le\bar\rho_{\Delta T_2}$ by \eqref{eq:gk} and Lemma~\ref{lem:rhobar}(i), and
$\bar\rho_{h_k+w_k}\le\bar\rho_{\Delta T_1+\Delta T_2}$, $\bar\rho_{h_k}\le\bar\rho_{\Delta T_1}$ by
Lemma~\ref{lem:rhobar}(ii); the sum has $|\mathcal I_R|$ terms. The value at $\pstar$ is
Lemma~\ref{lem:rhobar}(iv) and the monotonicity in $\delta$ is Lemma~\ref{lem:rhobar}(iii).
\end{proof}
\readthis{four levels, each obtained from the one above by a single named enlargement, and each
reversible by stepping back.
\emph{(a)} \eqref{eq:prop_removal_datum} is the sharpest statement the Gr\"onwall machinery of
\S\ref{sec:lemmas} supports: the actual $\mu$, the actual per-datum offsets, the two residual terms
kept separately, and the \emph{realised} noise at each launch node and each datum rather than the

$\pstar$ are unknown in a discovery problem. The evaluable object is the score of
\eqref{eq:score} below, which is a different thing and is not claimed to be this one.
\emph{(b)} \eqref{eq:prop_removal_node} substitutes $\norm\eta_{\max}$ for realised noise;

\emph{(c)} \eqref{eq:prop_removal} is the form used in Theorem~\ref{thm:main} and
```

#### (D5) The node-selection rule, observable score and honest costs [replaces R4 block (H) in full]

```latex
\paragraph{Node selection: what is computable, and what it costs.}
For a proposed removal set $R$ (a subset of the interior fine nodes) write $\tau_k^{-}(R)$ for the
nearest node of $S\setminus R$ to the left of $\tau_k$, $w_k(R)=\tau_k-\tau_k^{-}(R)$,
$g_k(R;p)=\norm{\flow{w_k(R)}{p}{y_{\tau_k^{-}(R)}}-y_{\tau_k}}$, and, for $t_i\in D_k$,
\[
a_i(R;p)=\big\lVert\flow{t_i-\tau_k^{-}(R)}{p}{y_{\tau_k^{-}(R)}}-y_i\big\rVert,
\qquad
b_i(p)=\big\lVert\flow{t_i-\tau_k}{p}{y_{\tau_k}}-y_i\big\rVert ,
\]
the \emph{proposed coarse} and \emph{current fine} residuals. The proof of
Proposition~\ref{prop:removal} bounds $|a_i^2-b_i^2|$ by $e^{\mu(t_i-\tau_k)}g_k(R;p)(a_i+b_i)$
before any residual is bounded by $r$, $\rho$ or $\bar\rho$, so
\begin{equation}
S(R;p)=\sum_{k\in R}\ \sum_{t_i\in D_k}
e^{\mu(t_i-\tau_k)}\,g_k(R;p)\,\big(a_i(R;p)+b_i(p)\big)\ \ge\ \big|\hatJ_K(p)-J_K(p)\big|
\label{eq:score}
\end{equation}
is a valid upper bound every factor of which is computed from quantities the integrator returns,
given a numerical value for $\mu$ (any upper bound on $\mu$ may be used, since $t_i\ge\tau_k$; $\mu$
is a constant of (A0) and is itself not instantiated in this report, see P1 in \S\ref{sec:prov}).
Two rules: \emph{(i)} score complete proposed removal sets by $S(R;p)$ at the current iterate and
take the smallest; \emph{(ii)} \emph{sequential greedy}: $R_0=\emptyset$ and
$R_j=R_{j-1}\cup\{\arg\min_c S(R_{j-1}\cup\{c\};p)\}$ over retained interior candidates $c$,
\emph{recomputing} $\tau_k^{-}$, $w_k$, $g_k$ and the $a_i$ of the whole current set after each
removal. Qualitatively, \eqref{eq:score} prefers nodes that launch few data ($|D_k|$ small), sit
close to their retained left neighbour ($w_k$ small), and above all have a small coarse residual
$g_k$.

\readthis{three things this paragraph now says that a previous revision did not.
\emph{(1) The score a previous revision proposed was not observable.} It read
$\sum\sum e^{\mu(t_i-\tau_k)}g_k(R;p)[\rho_{t_i-\tau_k^{-}(R)}(p)+\rho_{t_i-\tau_k}(p)]$, and
$\rho_s(p)=(1+e^{\mu s})\norm\eta_{\max}+Q(s)\norm{p-\pstar}$ contains the noise sup-norm and the
distance to the truth, neither of which exists in a discovery problem. Making $g_k$ observable does
not make the product observable. That form is withdrawn in favour of \eqref{eq:score}.
\emph{(2) The exact gap is observable at the same cost, and dominates the score for ranking.}
The integration that produces $a_i$ produces $a_i^2-b_i^2$, so
$\hatJ_K(p)-J_K(p)=\sum_{k\in R}\sum_{t_i\in D_k}(a_i^2-b_i^2)$ is available at exactly the cost of
\eqref{eq:score}, with no slack and no $\mu$. For ranking candidate removal sets \emph{at the current
iterate}, use it. \eqref{eq:score} and, above it, $\Delta_K$ of \eqref{eq:prop_removal} earn their
place only where a bound \emph{uniform over $q$ in a set} is required --- which is what
Theorem~\ref{thm:main} and Corollary~\ref{cor:basin} consume and what a pointwise evaluation cannot
supply.
\emph{(3) There is no set-independent per-node score.} $\tau_k^{-}$ is the nearest \emph{retained}
node, so removing one node changes $w_k$, $\tau_k^{-}$, $g_k$ and every $a_i$ for the
already-removed nodes in the same consecutive run to its right; a per-node term is a contribution
\emph{given the current retained set}, not a marginal cost that can be ranked once. Ranking once and
removing the top $|\mathcal I_R|$ can select a set whose bound is far from the sum of its
pre-removal scores, most severely for block removals --- the case of
Figure~\ref{fig:removal}(b).}

\paragraph{Cost.} Let $R$ be the current removal set and $C$ the number of retained interior
candidates. Evaluating \eqref{eq:score} for a given $R$ costs one coarse-window integration per
$k\in R$, over $[\tau_k^{-}(R),\max D_k]$ (length $w_k+h_k$), each of which yields $g_k$ and all the
$a_i$ of that node as by-products; the $b_i$ are residuals the fine objective already computes.
Hence:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item \textbf{per candidate $c$:} $O(1)$ coarse-window integrations for $c$ itself, \emph{plus} a
re-integration for every already-removed $k\in R$ whose retained left neighbour changes when $c$ is
removed --- none if $c$ is isolated from $R$, and the whole consecutive run to the right of $c$ in
the worst case, i.e.\ $O(|R|)$;
\item \textbf{per greedy step:} $O(C\,|R|)$ window integrations in the worst case, $O(C)$ when
removals stay isolated;
\item \textbf{whole greedy schedule} of $|\mathcal I_R|$ removals: $O(|\mathcal I_R|\,C\,|R|)
=O(C\,|\mathcal I_R|^{2})$ in the worst case.
\end{itemize}
\readthis{caching, which is a remark and not the headline figure. Removing $c$ changes
$\tau_k^{-}$ only for nodes in the removed run immediately to its right, so contributions cached by
the key $(\tau_k^{-}(R),\tau_k)$ remain valid for every other candidate and a greedy step
re-evaluates only the candidates whose left retained neighbour changed at the previous step ---
$O(1)$ candidates for isolated removals. The worst-case figures above stand. Standing caveat: all of
this minimises a \emph{bound} on the cost gap (or, for the exact gap, the cost gap itself at one
$p$), which is not the same as minimising the displacement of the minimiser; that step needs the
strong-convexity premise of Theorem~\ref{thm:main} and the reconnection hypothesis (R) of
Remark~\ref{rem:reconnect}.}
```
