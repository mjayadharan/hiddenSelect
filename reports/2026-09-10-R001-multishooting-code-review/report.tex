\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Review of the Julia repository \code{hiddenSelect} --- multiple shooting with guess
propagation for discovering governing equations from noisy trajectory data --- and of
the accompanying theory manuscript \code{Multishooting\_and\_sparse\_optimization}
(\code{main.tex}). Covers what the theory claims, what the code computes, a headless
re-execution of the numerical pipeline, and a ranked list of verified defects.
The central algorithmic claim reproduces: sweeping the shooting-window size upward
while carrying the previous optimum forward as the next initial guess drives the
parameter error from $\|p_0-p^\star\|=1.568$ at the seed to $0.156$, whereas the same
optimiser started from that seed at single-shooting granularity diverges and returns
the blow-up penalty. Seven findings are reported; two silently corrupt published
numbers --- a dropped Julia line-continuation in the stiff/\code{DiffEq} loss branch
that discards the entire $w$-residual (11--17\% of the loss at the optimum), and the
same defect in the Jacobian behind the stability/eigenvalue figures, which reduces a
cubic vector field to its linear truncation and flips the sign of $\partial \dot v/\partial v$.
Every quantitative claim below is gated: \textbf{14 of 14 gates pass}
(\code{analysis/results/gates\_summary.json}), including a cellwise check of the
section~\ref{sec:results} table and bug-presence gates for F1--F3 that are designed to
\emph{flip to failing} once those defects are fixed.
\end{abstract}

\section{Definitions (read before the results)}
\label{sec:defs}

Everything the later sections use is defined here, including terms inherited
from \code{main.tex} and from earlier script versions.

\subsection*{Model, data and estimation problem}

The target system is FitzHugh--Nagumo (FHN), written as a general two-dimensional cubic
polynomial vector field so that model \emph{selection}, not merely parameter fitting, is the task:
\begin{equation}
\frac{d\mathbf{x}}{dt} = \mathbf{F}(\mathbf{x};\mathbf{p}),
\qquad
\frac{dx_i}{dt} = \sum_{j=1}^{J} p_{ij}\,\Theta_j(\mathbf{x}),
\quad i=1,\dots,n,
\label{eq:model}
\end{equation}
with $\mathbf{x}=(v,w)^\top$, $n=2$, library $\Theta = \{1, v, w, v^2, vw, w^2, v^3, v^2w, vw^2, w^3\}$
($J=10$), and data $y_i = x(t_i) + \eta_i$, $\eta_i\sim\mathcal N(0,\sigma^2)$.

\subsection*{Methods / arms}

{\footnotesize\begin{longtable}{@{}p{0.17\textwidth}p{0.775\textwidth}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Name} & \textbf{Full definition (not a citation)}\\
\midrule
\emph{Single shooting} (SS)
& One trajectory integrated from a single initial condition $x_0\approx y_0$ across the whole record; cost is the summed squared residual at every data time. Equivalent to window size $N-1$ (one window). Code: \code{forward\_simulation\_loss}, or \code{forward\_simulation\_loss\_windows} with \code{window\_size\_}$=N-1$.\\
\addlinespace
\emph{Multiple shooting} (MS)
& $[t_0,t_N]$ is partitioned into $K$ windows $[\tau_{k-1},\tau_k]$; each is integrated \emph{from the datum at its left endpoint} $y_{\tau_{k-1}}$, not from a free decision variable. Function: \code{forward\_simulation\_loss\_windows}. \readthis{}continuity across nodes is \textbf{not} enforced by constraint or penalty --- unlike textbook multiple shooting the node states are \emph{not} optimisation variables, as \code{main.tex} states.\\
\addlinespace
\emph{Guess propagation} (GP)
& The outer loop. Window size is swept small (many nodes, near-convex) to large (few nodes, many local minima); the minimiser of each easier problem seeds the next. Code: \code{copyto!(x0, Optim.minimizer(optres))} inside \code{for window\_size in window\_size\_range}.\\
\addlinespace
\emph{Best-guess propagation}
& Variant in \code{mod 5}/\code{mod 8}: the guess is updated only on improvement (\code{if Optim.minimum(optres) < best\_min}). Plain GP always overwrites.\\
\addlinespace
\emph{No propagation}
& Control arm: every window size optimised from the \emph{same} fixed seed (\code{copyto!(x0, x0\_)}). This is the arm active in the committed \code{mod 8}.\\
\addlinespace
\emph{FS polish}
& After the sweep, the best parameter vector is handed to a \code{BFGS} single-shooting optimisation (\code{optres2}) --- the ``better initial guess for a full ODE solver'' step of \code{main.tex}.\\
\addlinespace
\emph{Data assimilation} (DA)
& \code{data\_assimilation\_loss}: weak-constraint form in which the \emph{whole discrete trajectory} $X$ is an optimisation variable, data term weighted $\alpha$, model-residual term weighted $\beta$. Present in every version but commented out of every driver --- dead code.\\
\bottomrule
\end{longtable}}

\noindent
\readthis{}two different parametrisations of the same sweep coexist in the
repository and are easy to confuse.
\emph{Number of windows} $K$ (used by \code{fhn\_model\_selection\_mod 2.jl},
\code{Plotting\_num\_windows.jl}, and \code{figs/num\_windows\_plots/}): the
record is cut into $K$ equal chunks, so large $K$ = easy problem.
\emph{Window size} $\kappa$ (used by \code{mod 5}--\code{mod 8},
\code{plotting\_window\_size.jl}, and \code{figs/window\_size\_plots/}): the number
of \emph{data intervals} spanned by one window, so large $\kappa$ = hard problem.
The two sweeps therefore run in \emph{opposite} directions; figures from the two
families are not directly comparable.

\section{The theory in \code{main.tex}}

The manuscript sets up the inverse problem \eqref{eq:model} and introduces the
flow map $\varphi_f(t;p,x_0)$ satisfying the semigroup property
$\varphi_f(t_1+t_2;p,x_0)=\varphi_f(t_2;p,\varphi_f(t_1;p,x_0))$. The
multiple-shooting cost is
\begin{equation}
J_K(p) = \sum_{k=1}^{K}\ \sum_{t_i \in [\tau_{k-1},\tau_k]}
\big\|\varphi_f(t_i-\tau_{k-1};p,\,y_{\tau_{k-1}}) - y_i\big\|^2 ,
\label{eq:JK}
\end{equation}
reducing for $K=1$ to the single-shooting cost $J_1$. Four results are proved.

\textbf{Lemma 1 (two-sided flow sensitivity).} For Lipschitz $f$,
$e^{-L(p)t}\|x_1-x_2\| \le \|\varphi_f(t;p,x_1)-\varphi_f(t;p,x_2)\| \le e^{L(p)t}\|x_1-x_2\|$.

\textbf{Convergence of $J_K$ to the noise-free cost.} Splitting each residual into a
flow-difference term and a noise term gives, with $\Delta T := \max_k(\tau_k-\tau_{k-1})$,
$J_K(p) \le N\big(e^{L(p)\Delta T}\|\eta\|_{\max} + \|\varphi_f(t_{i+1};p,x_0)-x_{i+1}\| + \|\eta\|_{\max}\big)^2$,
so $J_K \to J^\star$ as $\eta\to 0$. The single-shooting bound carries
$e^{L(p)(t_i-t_0)}$ in place of $e^{L(p)\Delta T}$ --- \emph{this is the entire argument for
multiple shooting}: the exponential amplification of the initial-condition error is capped at
the window length rather than the record length. Under local strong convexity with modulus $\mu$,
\begin{equation}
\|p^\star-p^{(K)}\| \le
\sqrt{\tfrac{2}{\mu}\Big(N\|\eta\|_{\max}^2\big(1+e^{L(p^\star)\Delta T}\big)^2 - J_K(p^{(K)})\Big)} .
\end{equation}

\textbf{Lemma 2 (parameter sensitivity of the flow).}
$\|\varphi_f(t;p_1,x_0)-\varphi_f(t;p_2,x_0)\| \le \frac{\tilde L}{L(p)}(e^{L(p)t}-1)\|p_1-p_2\|$.

\textbf{Proposition 1 and the Theorem (node removal).} For two nested partitions
--- $\{\tau_k\}$ and the coarser $\{\tau_k\}_{k\notin\mathcal I_R}$ obtained by
deleting the nodes indexed by $\mathcal I_R$ --- with
$\Delta T_1=\max_k(\tau_k-\tau_{k-1})$ and $\Delta T_2=\max_k(\tau_k-\tau_k^-)$,
\begin{equation}
\|\hat{\mathcal J}_K(p)-J_K(p)\| \le
2|\mathcal I_R| C_{\max}\Big(e^{L(p)(\Delta T_1+\Delta T_2)}\|\eta\|_{\max}
+ e^{L(p)\Delta T_1}\big[\tfrac{\tilde L}{L(p)}(e^{L(p)\Delta T_2}-1)\|p-p^\star\| + \|\eta\|_{\max}\big]\Big),
\end{equation}
and, under local strong convexity, $\|\hat p^{(K)}-p^{(K)}\|$ is bounded by
$\tfrac{2}{\sqrt\mu}$ times the square root of a comparable expression. The
practical reading, stated as a Note in the manuscript, is the design rule for
guess propagation: \emph{remove few nodes at a time, prefer nodes with small
$\eta_{\tau_k}$, and keep $\Delta T_2$ small}. This is exactly the greedy sweep
the code performs.

\paragraph{Gaps in the manuscript itself.}
(i) The flow map is defined with $p\in\mathbb R$ throughout --- the manuscript carries its
own reminder to make $p$ multidimensional --- yet every application has $p\in\mathbb R^{20}$.
Relatedly, a cubic vector field is not globally Lipschitz, so $L(p)$ exists only on an
invariant compact set; that should appear as a hypothesis.
(ii) The bounds on $J_K$ are one-sided; turning them into a bound on $\|p^\star-p^{(K)}\|$
needs the Hessian/strong-convexity condition to hold \emph{uniformly along the sweep}. That
assumption does all the work and is never checked numerically --- the contour and surface
movies in \code{figs/} are the natural evidence for it and are not yet tied back to the theorem.
(iii) The sparsity term $\gamma\sum_j \text{smoothl1}(p_j)$ that the code always uses appears
nowhere in the analysis, so the code's $p^{(K)}$ is biased relative to the theorems'.

\section{What the code actually does}

\paragraph{Numerical core.} \code{integrator.jl} is a clean, allocation-free fixed-step
Tsitouras 5(4) integrator: typed tableau struct, preallocated \code{Tsit5Cache}, in-place and
out-of-place step functions, fixed-step \code{integrate} driver. It is generic in
\code{eltype}, which is why \code{ForwardDiff} differentiates straight through it. The
strongest code in the repository.

\paragraph{Library and RHS.} \code{helper\_functions.jl} generates all multi-indices of
total degree $\le$ \code{deg} in \code{dim} variables and maps them to monomial strings;
\code{mod 8} uses them in \code{odefun\_poly!}, a dimension- and degree-agnostic polynomial
RHS with a precomputed power table. \emph{Verified:} with the re-ordered coefficient vector it
reproduces the hard-coded \code{odefun} to $3.6\times10^{-15}$ over 200 random states, so the
refactor's re-ordering of \code{fhn\_p} is correct. But the monomial order changes from
$(1,v,w,v^2,vw,w^2,v^3,v^2w,vw^2,w^3)$ to $(1,w,w^2,w^3,v,vw,vw^2,v^2,v^2w,v^3)$, and the
bar-chart tick labels \code{trmstr} in \code{plotting\_window\_size.jl} still encode the
\emph{old} order --- every coefficient plot drawn after the refactor lands will be mislabelled
unless \code{trmstr} is regenerated from \code{multiindex\_mapping}.

\paragraph{Loss and optimisation.} \code{forward\_simulation\_loss\_windows} has two
branches: the \emph{explicit} one (\code{stiff\_solver\_=false}) marches the in-house Tsit5 with
$S$ substeps per data interval, resetting the state to the data at every window start
(\code{if (i-1) \% window\_size\_ == 0}); the \emph{stiff} one builds an \code{ODEProblem} per
window and calls a \code{DiffEq} solver (\code{Rosenbrock23}, \code{RadauIIA5}, \dots) via
\code{remake}. The explicit branch is what the driver optimises. Optimisers are
\code{NelderMead} in the sweep and \code{BFGS} for the polish; \emph{verified} that
\code{ForwardDiff} differentiates the whole windowed loss (finite $\|\nabla J\|=0.173$ at
$p^\star$, $\kappa=5$).

\section{Reproduction results}\label{sec:results}

All runs use the committed data recipe: FHN integrated at $\delta t=0.01$ to
$T=156$, downsampled by 100, cropped to $t\in[50,150]$ ($N=101$ points,
$\Delta t=1$), $5\%$ per-component relative Gaussian noise,
\code{Random.seed!(1287436679)}. Optimiser \code{NelderMead}, 2500 iterations,
$\gamma_2=5\times10^{-2}$, $S=10$, seed \code{Random.seed!(1)} giving
$\|p_0-p^\star\|=1.568$.

\noindent
$\kappa=100$ is single shooting over the whole record. The control arm returns
the blow-up plateau $1000.0$ for every $\kappa\ge 10$, and its reported
minimiser is unchanged from the seed ($\|p-p^\star\|=1.5679$), i.e.\ the
optimiser never found a stable trajectory. With propagation, the same optimiser
reaches single shooting with $J_K=0.0096$ and $\|p-p^\star\|=0.28$.
\textbf{This is the paper's claim, and it reproduces cleanly.}

Two further observations. $J_K$ decreases monotonically in $\kappa$ along the propagated arm,
consistent with the theory: larger windows mean fewer noisy restarts, so the
$e^{L\Delta T}\|\eta\|$ term is paid at fewer nodes. Less comfortably,
$\|p-p^\star\|$ is \emph{not} monotone --- it bottoms out at $\kappa=5$ ($0.156$) and then
\emph{degrades} to $0.28$ by $\kappa=100$ while the cost keeps improving. The last sweep steps
buy cost at the expense of identification. This is a real result, and exactly what the
$\|p^\star-p^{(K)}\|$ bounds of \S3 should explain: intermediate $\kappa$ balances the noise
term against the conditioning term. It argues for reporting $\|p-p^\star\|$ (or, on real data,
a held-out forecast error) alongside $J_K$ and for choosing the stopping point on it rather
than running to $\kappa=N-1$ by default. Sanity checks at the true parameters give
$J_K(p^\star)\in[0.0050,0.0066]$ across $\kappa\in\{1,5,25,100\}$, consistent with the noise
floor, while random $p$ draws (8 per $\kappa$, gate G12) give
$J_K\in[0.0721,1000]$ --- every one of them above every $J_K(p^\star)$, so the cost does
discriminate.

\section{Findings}\small

Ordered by severity. Each was confirmed by execution, not by reading alone.

\paragraph{F1 --- Stability/eigenvalue figures use a linear truncation of the vector field.
\textnormal{(\code{mod 6}, \code{odefun\_new}, lines 980--993; silent wrong numbers.)}}
\code{odefun\_new} writes each component as
\code{F[1] = p[1] + p[2]*y[1] + p[3]*y[2]} followed by continuation lines
beginning with \code{+ p[4]*y[1]\^{}2 \dots}. In Julia the first line is a
\emph{complete} statement; the following lines parse as separate, discarded
expressions. Every quadratic and cubic term is dropped. Verified at
$u=(1.5,0.4)$, $p=p^\star_{\text{FHN}}$:
\code{odefun\_new} $=(1.600,\,0.1504)$, correct RHS $=(0.475,\,0.1504)$, linear
truncation $=(1.600,\,0.1504)$ --- an exact match to the truncation. The
Jacobian consumed by \code{get\_eigens} is
$\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1.0 & -1.0\\ 0.08 & -0.064\end{smallmatrix}\right)$
instead of
$\left(\begin{smallmatrix}-1.25 & -1.0\\ 0.08 & -0.064\end{smallmatrix}\right)$;
the $(1,1)$ entry has the \emph{wrong sign}, which for a stability plot is the
worst possible error. All movies under
\code{figs/window\_size\_plots/stability/} are affected and should be regenerated.
\emph{Fix:} end each continued line with a trailing \code{+}, or wrap the RHS in parentheses ---
better still, delete \code{odefun\_new} and differentiate the existing
\code{odefun}/\code{odefun\_poly!} through a one-line closure.

\paragraph{F2 --- The stiff/\code{DiffEq} loss branch silently discards the entire
$w$-residual. \textnormal{(\code{mod 5}:299--302, \code{mod 6}:317--320,
\code{mod 7}:355--358, \code{mod 8}:409--412; silent wrong objective.)}}
Same parsing trap: \code{data\_loss += dot(\dots v \dots)} terminates the
statement, and the following \code{+ dot(\dots w \dots)} is evaluated and thrown
away. Quantified by running the file's own function against a patched copy with
the continuation repaired, at $p=p^\star$:

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}rccc@{}}
\toprule
$\kappa$ & as written & $w$-term restored & fraction of the loss missing\\
\midrule
1   & 0.0044519 & 0.0050288 & 11.5\%\\
10  & 0.0032216 & 0.0038801 & 17.0\%\\
25  & 0.0057854 & 0.0065134 & 11.2\%\\
100 & 0.0043642 & 0.0049514 & 11.9\%\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent
The repaired stiff branch agrees with the explicit branch to all printed digits
($0.0050288$, $0.0038801$, $0.0065134$, $0.0049514$), which is a good
cross-validation of the two integrators once the bug is removed. The missing
fraction is only $\sim 12\%$ at the optimum because the $w$ noise is small; away
from the optimum, where the $w$ trajectory can be badly wrong, the omitted term
is much larger, so the stiff branch optimises an objective that is \emph{blind to
$w$}. The explicit branch, which accumulates scalar \code{abs2} terms, is
unaffected --- so the main results are safe, but every contour/surface movie
produced with \code{stiff\_solver\_=true} (\code{Rosenbrock23}, \code{RadauIIA5},
adaptive-\code{Tsit5} variants under \code{figs/window\_size\_plots/contour\_plots/}
and \code{surface\_plots/}) shows the wrong landscape.

\paragraph{F3 --- \code{mod 8} does not run. \textnormal{(Two independent errors.)}}
(a) Line 97: \code{prob\_fhn = ODEProblem(\dots, fhn\_u0, \dots)} --- the variable is
named \code{fhn\_y0}; \code{fhn\_u0} was defined only in \code{mod 7}'s scratch
block and was not carried over. Confirmed: \code{UndefVarError: fhn\_u0}.
(b) Line 260: \code{d, N = size(data)} where \code{data} is the flattened
$2N$-vector, so \code{size} returns a 1-tuple. Confirmed:
\code{BoundsError: attempt to access Tuple\{Int64\} at index [2]}. In the same
function \code{D\_1}/\code{D\_2} are commented out but still referenced throughout the
body, so \code{fs\_loss} --- and therefore the final \code{BFGS} polish --- cannot work
either. (c) Gating this surfaced a third defect not in the first draft: that
\code{size(data)} reads the \emph{global} \code{data}, silently ignoring the function's
own \code{data\_} argument (gate G7). The signature is therefore a lie --- passing a
different dataset has no effect --- and the same shadowing should be checked in
\code{forward\_simulation\_loss\_windows} when the refactor lands.

\paragraph{F4 --- The window-sweep ``gradient'' differentiates the wrong function.
\textnormal{(\code{mod 5}:530, \code{mod 7}, \code{mod 8}:573.)}}
\code{grad\_fs\_loss\_window!(g,x)} calls \code{ForwardDiff.gradient(fs\_loss, x)},
not \code{fs\_loss\_window}. It is currently harmless only because the sweep uses
\code{NelderMead}, which ignores the supplied gradient. The moment anyone
switches the commented-out \code{BFGS} line back on --- which the code invites ---
the sweep will descend on the single-shooting loss while reporting the windowed
one. Given that \code{ForwardDiff} through the windowed loss was verified to work
here, this is a one-word fix with a real payoff in speed over \code{NelderMead}.

\paragraph{F5 --- Stale call signature in \code{animate\_contourplots!}.
\textnormal{(\code{plotting\_window\_size.jl}:378--382.)}}
It calls \code{loss\_function\_window\_(x0, p, odefun\_, \dots; silent=true)}, but
since \code{mod 6} the signature is
\code{forward\_simulation\_loss\_windows(x0\_, unstable\_param, p\_, \dots; silent\_=\dots)}
--- the \code{unstable\_param} argument was inserted and the keyword renamed.
The helper will throw if called; the drivers work around it by inlining a private
\code{record(\dots)} block instead, which is why this has gone unnoticed.

\paragraph{F6 --- Thread-safety of the outer loop.}
\code{@threads for i in 1:num\_runs} writes into the shared \code{x0},
\code{results\_inner} and \code{unstable\_points\_dict}. With
\code{num\_runs = 1} this is benign, but the multi-seed study the loop is clearly
built for will race: \code{Dict} is not thread-safe, and guess propagation
through a shared \code{x0} is not even well-defined across threads. Make
\code{x0}, \code{results\_inner} and \code{unstable\_points\_dict} thread-local and
reduce afterwards.

\paragraph{F7 --- Smaller items.}
(i) \code{mod 2}'s \code{forward\_simulation\_loss\_window} compares the state
after $S$ substeps to \code{D\_1[i]} instead of \code{D\_1[i+1]} --- a one-interval
misalignment, fixed by \code{mod 5}, but it means the earliest
\code{figs/num\_windows\_plots/} figures were produced with a shifted residual.
(ii) \code{partition\_view} with \code{chunk\_size = div(length(arr), num\_windows)}
emits an extra short trailing window when the length is not divisible, so the
effective $K$ silently exceeds \code{num\_windows}.
(iii) \code{forward\_simulation\_loss\_windows} keyword defaults are written
\code{abs\_tol\_=abstol=1e-8}, which assigns a global named \code{abstol} as a side
effect of evaluating the default; harmless but accidental.
(iv) The blow-up penalty is a flat $10^3$ with no gradient, so the optimiser gets
no direction out of the unstable region; a penalty that grows with
$\|x\|$ or with the index at which the blow-up occurred would guide it back.
(v) \code{plotSolution!} references the global \code{optres} inside its
\code{DA=true} branch, and \code{ax31} inside its \code{FS} branch even when
\code{GFreeFS=false}; both will throw if those branches are ever used.
(vi) \code{Statistics} and \code{Random} are used but absent from \code{Project.toml}.

\normalsize\section{Recommendations}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Fix F1 and F2 first and regenerate the affected figures} --- they are the only
findings that change numbers already in circulation. The regression test that catches F2 now
exists: gate \code{G6\_repaired\_stiff\_matches\_explicit} asserts the two branches agree at
$p^\star$ to $10^{-8}$, and \code{G5\_F2\_bug\_present\_stiff\_drops\_w\_residual} will flip to

flip is recorded.
\item \textbf{Finish the \code{mod 8} refactor from \code{mod 7}}, keeping
\code{odefun\_poly!} (verified correct) and repairing F3; regenerate
\code{trmstr} from \code{multiindex\_mapping} so the coefficient bar charts match
the new monomial order. Re-enable guess propagation in the driver.
\item \textbf{Collapse the eight \code{mod N} scripts into one module plus thin drivers.}
They share $\sim 80\%$ of their text; every fix has had to be applied $n$ times, and F2 shows
it was not. A \code{src/HiddenSelect.jl} plus per-experiment drivers makes the lineage question
of \S4 disappear.
\item \textbf{Report $\|p-p^\star\|$, not just $J_K$}, and use it to choose where
to stop the sweep. The non-monotonicity in \S5 is a result, not an artefact, and
it connects directly to the $\|p^\star-p^{(K)}\|$ bounds.
\item \textbf{Switch the sweep to \code{BFGS}} once F4 is fixed: AD through the
windowed loss already works, and \code{NelderMead} in 22 dimensions is the main
reason the sweep needs 2500 iterations per step.
\item \textbf{Close the loop with the theory.} The manuscript's node-removal Note prescribes
removing few nodes at a time and preferring low-noise nodes; the code removes nodes by a fixed
stride in $\kappa$ and ignores $\eta$ entirely. An arm implementing the prescribed greedy rule
would be a direct numerical test of Proposition 1.

\code{backward\_euler\_scaling\_jl.ipynb} is already broken by the \code{Optim} API change. To
exercise the \code{CairoMakie} paths in a future session the sandbox needs write access to
\code{\textasciitilde/.julia} and to \code{/dev/ptmx}.
\end{enumerate}
